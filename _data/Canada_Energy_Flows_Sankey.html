How to read it. On the left are the fuels Canadians use at home: electricity from hydro, nuclear, wind and solar, and fossil fuels. Some fossil fuel is burned in power plants (the middle box). On the right is where the energy ends up: homes, businesses, industry, vehicles, the energy industry's own use, and materials such as plastics feedstock. The grey band is energy lost as heat when fuel is burned to make electricity.

Method and caveats

  • Source: Statistics Canada, table 25-10-0029-01, retrieved November 2026 through Statistics Canada's Web Data Service. Values are converted from terajoules to petajoules (1 PJ = 1,000 TJ) and rounded. 2025 is the latest year published.
  • Electricity from fuels: fuel burned in power plants (by utilities and industry) flows into the Electricity box. What comes out is Statistics Canada's "secondary electricity, thermal". The difference is the conversion loss.
  • Hydro, nuclear, wind & solar: Statistics Canada reports these together as "primary electricity". The band equals electricity used in the region minus thermal generation, so it is domestic use after trade and grid losses. Electricity exports and imports are listed in the totals.
  • Oil products are refined products as used (gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, heavy fuel oil…). Refinery losses and crude-oil exports are not drawn. NGLs are natural gas liquids (propane, ethane, butane), mostly used as petrochemical feedstock.
  • Commercial & institutional includes public administration. "Energy industry own use" is Statistics Canada's "producer consumption". The statistical difference is left out.
  • Suppressed values: Statistics Canada withholds some provincial cells for confidentiality. They are counted as zero, so small provinces may be slightly understated.
  • This chart covers domestic energy use. Canada also produces far more energy than it uses: most crude oil and natural gas is exported (see the export figure above the chart).
